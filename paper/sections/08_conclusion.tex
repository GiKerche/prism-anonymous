\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
PRISM shows that an activation direction estimated once in a BF16
model transfers along three axes. Across precision, native--shadow paired differences stay within
$1.8$ pp without establishing equivalence; NF4 improves direction
availability in sparse Qwen and Ministral subsets.
Across languages, mean native induction is preserved in Qwen3.5-9B
and roughly halved in Llama-3.1-8B; the five-language direction also
acts in Arabic, which supplied no estimation or selection data. Across scale, a
linear map fitted on benign inputs carries the direction into a
model up to nine times larger, raising unsafe calls by $3.62$ to $76.01$ pp
and lowering them by $7.65$ and $35.15$ pp, with BFCL changes from
$-2.84$ to $+1.20$ pp. Transfer is uneven across language pairs,
receiver-native directions remain competitive, and effect sizes depend
on the judge, with no human reference: PRISM establishes transfer under
the evaluated conditions and delimits its generality.
