\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Behavioral steering and tool safety}
Contrastive activation addition derives steering vectors from representation differences \citep{panickssery2024steering}; refusal-direction interventions connect such geometry to safety behavior \citep{arditi2024refusal}. AgentLens extends internal control to coding agents through detection and intervention on execution-step representations \citep{luo2026agentlens}. Tool-calling introduces a distinct endpoint: textual refusals can coexist with prohibited calls \citep{cartagena2026gap}, and tool identity itself is linearly readable and steerable \citep{wu2026linear}. Interaction history also matters: SODA finds improved safety after preceding benign agentic tasks \citep{sun2026soda}. PRISM studies isolated call proposals, fitting directions from observed unsafe-action frequencies and evaluating their transfer under fixed interventions. We measure semantic harm, call validity, and benign capability separately. These measurements alone do not isolate a safety-specific representation from general tool-calling behavior.

\subsection{Compression as behavioral supervision}
\citet{chhabra2025compressed} investigate refusal mechanisms after
compression and use mechanistic interventions to improve compressed-model safety. Q-resafe assesses quantization-related safety risks and repairs quantized checkpoints through precision-aware patching \citep{chen2025qresafe}. PRISM uses the compressed copy as a source of labels: NF4 behavior supervises directions fitted and applied in the original BF16 model. Paired label-yield and scarce-label analyses test when this auxiliary supervision is useful; NF4 need not weaken alignment in every model. Weak-to-strong jailbreaking also uses smaller models to influence an aligned target, by modifying its decoding probabilities \citep{zhao2024weakstrong}. In PRISM, auxiliary behavior is used during direction estimation; the fitted vector then acts on the target's activations.

\subsection{Transfer across languages}
\citet{wang2025refusaluniversal} show that refusal directions from
safety-aligned languages transfer across languages. BabelSteering applies English-derived refusal vectors to eight languages while measuring refusal of harmful requests, over-refusal, and general task utility \citep{stein2026babel}. PRISM tests whether directions conditioned on tool actions transfer across five source and target languages and from their pooled information to Arabic, which is excluded from fitting and tuning. Arabic translates 11 scenarios from the final set, so this evaluates a held-out language on shared tasks. These behavioral effects do not establish uniform language transfer or selective multilingual safety; the benign BFCL controls are English-only.

\subsection{Transfer across model scales}
Learned activation mappings can transport safety interventions between models \citep{oozeer2025activation}. \citet{bello2025linear} test affine representation mappings that preserve steering effects from smaller to larger models. PRISM evaluates this transfer for harmful tool actions, learning a PCA--ridge--lift map from paired benign tool-use activations; harmful prompts and refusal labels do not enter map fitting. Receiver layers and doses use separate development partitions, so transport requires access to receiver activations and development outcomes. Experiments cover Qwen3.5-9B$\rightarrow$27B, Ministral3-8B$\rightarrow$14B, and Llama3.1-8B$\rightarrow$70B. The feasibility of transporting activation-based interventions across models has already been demonstrated in prior work. Our empirical question is whether such transfer extends to harmful tool-call proposals, how its effects vary across the three axes, and how it affects performance on tasks that require tool use.
