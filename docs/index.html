How to read the labels and the numbers
“Violation” means that a technically valid, state-changing or externally consequential tool call advances the harmful request. A refusal in the surrounding text does not cancel that call. “No violation” concerns this action criterion, not every possible harm in the text. “Unlabeled” means no valid judgment was available.
Rate denominators contain resolved labels. Paired effects use only pairs with both labels, so the effect can differ from a subtraction of the two marginal rates. Intervals resample entire scenario families and condition on the fitted interventions and recorded labels. The main comparisons also report multiplicity-adjusted intervals. Model-based judgments can be wrong.
The viewer retrieves saved responses. It does not run a model or execute a tool. A selected example illustrates one outcome; use the aggregate results above to assess an intervention.

01
The unit is model-specific.
The dimensionless dose a is multiplied by a fixed activation scale σ. Because d has unit length, the shift has length |a|σ. Changing the dose changes the distance along the direction.
02
A constrained choice.
A dose must lose at most five percentage points of BFCL accuracy on tool-required validation tasks and meet response-quality limits. The smallest admissible dose within one bootstrap standard error of the best gain is selected.
03
Selection and test are separate.
The curve measures a structural call proxy on Validation II. Final harmful-action effects use semantic judgments on separate test scenarios. There are no interpolated or newly generated outcomes in this viewer.

    


    

      
04 / THE IDEA
A change inside.
An action outside.
PRISM tests whether activation directions associated with harmful tool use can change behavior—and whether their effects survive a change of supervision, language, or model scale.

WHAT CHANGES
Behavior, not model weights.
Both native and shadow directions are estimated and applied in BF16 activation space. The NF4 copy contributes response labels only. Its activations are not transported into BF16.
NF4 labels improve direction availability in sparse construction subsets for Qwen and Ministral. The study does not establish that shadow effects are superior or equivalent to native effects.
WHAT THE EVIDENCE DOES NOT SETTLE
The limits are part of the result.
Calling-propensity controls closely align with the harmful-action directions. The results do not identify a unique, isolated safety mechanism. Effects depend on the model, language, and evaluator.
LUMEN measures proposed calls. SODA separately tests executed actions in simulated environments, with its own protocol and endpoint. This explorer displays LUMEN responses.
